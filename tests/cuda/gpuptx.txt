gpuptx: the PTX tests on a real GPU (tests/cuda/gpuptx.sh, gpuptx.c), and what is known to differ.
Last run: RTX PRO 4000 Blackwell (sm_120), driver 595, CUDA 13.2, x86_64 Linux. 18 of the 23 tests print
exactly their .exp: all nine g_* (every instruction of gen.py: integers, f32/f64 in the four rounding modes,
cvt with .sat/.ftz and what is not a number, the 8- and 4-bit formats, mma in every shape with the
block-scaled ones, ldmatrix) and t_atom t_basic t_call t_ext t_forms t_half t_mem t_par t_sync.

The five that differ, and must:
  t_bad1 t_bad2 t_bad3   wrong PTX. Both refuse it, with words of their own; the hardware's assembler at the
                         load, the engine at the load (t_bad1) or the launch (218, the others).
  t_diag                 what the engine says of kernels that go wrong (a bad address, trap, a barrier nobody
                         else reaches, ...). The device does not load the module at all: the assembler knows no
                         "frobnicate" and wants the thread count of bar.sync to be a multiple of 32.
  t_lib                  calls the engine's own library (__mcuda_*, printf): there is none on the device. And the
                         assembler wants a function declared with a result to be called with one; the engine
                         takes the call both ways.

Where the hardware was found to differ from what the tests first said, and what was done:
 the engine was wrong (changed, the tests with it)
  testp.normal           true for +0 and -0.
  fns.b32                a base above 31 gives 0xffffffff (the engine took base & 31).
  cvt to an integer of what is not a number: 0 only from .f32 to 32 bits or fewer; from .f64, or to 64 bits,
                         the top bit of the type (0x80000000, 0x8000000000000000; .s8 -128, .u8 128, .s16
                         -32768 from .f64). The PTX manual says 0.
  activemask, ldmatrix with a 32-bit .shared address: see the lead's tests tc.cu and intr.cu.
 the test was wrong (changed)
  cvt.sat between integers where the destination holds every source value (u64.u32, s32.s32, ...): refused.
  mma ... .tf32: the registers of A and B are .b32, not .f32.     ldmatrix .x1: the destination is {%r}.
  cp.async: the size of the copy is a constant (4, 8, 16); only the source's size may be a register.
  ldu: not on .local.      sub.bf16 and its like want sm_90, the fp8 conversions sm_89, e2m1 and the
  block-scaled mma sm_120a with .version 9.1 (4X with .ue8m0), so those modules now say so.
  A pointer from alloca, or the address of a .local, is a local address: ld.local/st.local, or cvta.local
  first. A plain ld/st of it is CUDA_ERROR_ILLEGAL_ADDRESS. (The engine takes both.)
  Every access must be at a multiple of its size, a vector's being that of the whole vector
  (CUDA_ERROR_MISALIGNED_ADDRESS). (The engine does not look.)
 different, and left so (the tests no longer show it)
  Constants. The assembler works out at build time what it can, and not always as the device would:
  sub.rn.f32 of 1.5 and 16777215 as two literals gave another last bit than the instruction run on the
  device, which agrees with the engine. gen.py now passes every constant through an xor with a zero read
  from memory.
  Very long kernels. cvt.rni.s32.f64 and cvt.rzi.u32.f64 of +inf gave 0 in a kernel of 10270 lines and the
  largest number, as they must, in any shorter cut of it. gen.py now ends a kernel after about 1500 lines.
  %nwarpid is 48 whatever the block (the engine: the block's warps). %total_smem_size is rounded up (128 for
  32 static + 64 dynamic bytes; the engine: 96), and shared addresses start at 0x400 (the engine: 0).
  The texts of errors, and when wrong PTX is refused (above).
  The sums of a floating-point mma. g_mma agrees with the device because its sums are exact. Where they are
  not, the device cuts where the engine rounds (the engine adds in double precision and rounds to the nearest
  once): of random f16 data about half the words differ, mostly by one in the last place, more where terms
  cancel. This is left so by default, because the cut is a property of one generation of the hardware and
  the rounded sum is the better one; MCUDA_MMA_HW=1 in the environment (mc_mma_hw(1) in a program, a line
  "//! hw" in a test for ptxt) makes the engine cut as the device does, interpreter and JIT alike.
  tests/cuda/mmahw.py says exactly what the device does, and tests/cuda/hw/mma.txt has 18688 of its words
  (146 sets of random data with full significands, wide and narrow ranges of exponents, zeros, subnormal
  numbers, sums that cancel), all of which that model gives; h_mma.ptx is those sets as a test that runs with
  the switch, and the device prints the same for it. Modelled, and every word the device's: m16n8k16 and
  m16n8k8 of f16 with f32 and f16 sums, bf16, tf32 (m16n8k8, m16n8k4), e4m3 and e5m2 (m16n8k32), and the
  block-scaled kinds: mxf8f6f4 1X with e4m3, e5m2, e2m3, e3m2 and e2m1 elements, mxf4 2X, mxf4nvf4 2X and 4X
  (those of e2m1 in four bits add sixteen products exactly before they cut, 35 bits below the largest scale).
  Not modelled (the default's sum in both modes): a sum with an infinity or a NaN among its terms or scales;
  its result was only looked at without scales. The cost of the switch: 2.2 times the time of a loop of
  m16n8k16 f16 mma (an assembly routine for that shape; the others go through C: 8 to 10 times when that shape was made to).
  Without the switch only this much is the device's: a NaN from an mma that is not block-scaled is
  0x7fffffff (0x7fff in .f16), and a sum that is zero is +0 whatever the signs.
  e2m1 in the bytes of mxf8f6f4 is bits 2 to 5 of the byte; the device reads bits 0 to 5 as e2m3, which is the
  same number when bits 0 and 1 are zero and another when not. The engine now reads it as the device does.
Not looked at here: NaN payloads beyond the canonical one, the approximate instructions (rcp.approx, sin,
...), which the generated tests do not use.
